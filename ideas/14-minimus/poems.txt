# Minimus

## January

Muffles on. Bells up. The year comes round
to its last ten minutes. The old man's hand
is on the tenor rope. The tower is still.
Look to. And we ring the old year home:

open, hush, open, hush. Then every hand
up for the muffles, and the bells go round
clean for the new one. Then four ways home
through frost, the bells behind us, still.

## February

Sleet. Practice night. A learner's hand
under his on the sally, and it comes home
to them both. Above, the bell goes round
and over, and the stay holds it, still.

You never see the bell. It's up there, home
on its wheel in the dark. All in the hand,
he says. When to pull. When to hold still.
And half a ton of bronze coming round.

## March

He lives at the end of the lane alone. Home
is a chair, a stove, her photograph still
on the sill, a dog's head in his hand,
and a mantel clock he winds, that goes round

and strikes, and then the house is still.
Tuesdays, he's the first one home
to the tower, up the stair, and first round
the ropes, checking each one with his hand.

## April

Good Friday, by custom, the bells are still.
Easter they're not. Six, and the sun round
the hill's shoulder. She's walked from home
in the dark, early, with the rope in hand.

Look to. Treble's going. She's gone. Round
and over, and her face is white and still
with counting, and the rope in her hand
is a live thing, and she brings it home.

## May

The swifts are back. Dusk. They scream round
the tower, and round, and are never still,
and only land to nest, and this is home.
If one comes down, you take it in your hand

and hold your arm up, open, and keep still,
till it drops, and catches, and is round
the tower again. Then a wedding. Every hand
to a rope; we ring the two of them home.

## June

Striking competition. The judges sit still
under the yew with clipboards. Every hand
shakes a little. The test piece goes round.
We come fourth. The pub is on the way home.

The longest day. The pub garden. His hand
on his glass has a shake. We keep still
about it. Light till ten. No one's home
before dark. The old man buys the round.

## July

His eightieth. We ring for it. The hand
on the tenor is the one that rang her home
half-muffled, years back, and it is still
the one that calls. Go, Plain Bob. Round

the twenty-four we go, and come home
at the twenty-fifth. That's all. Each hand
lets go. The hill sends it back round
a beat behind us, and then it's still.

## August

The swifts go in a night. Then, at home
in his chair, he does not come round.
The dog. The neighbour. A doctor's hand
on his wrist, and then the lane gone still.

Tuesday. Nobody rings. We stand round
the ropes and don't. His is tied up, home
on its hook, the bell above it still,
the sally worn to the shape of his hand.

## September

We tie the muffles on. His job. Round
one side of each clapper, and ring him home,
open, hush, open, hush, a borrowed hand
on his bell, until he's in. Then still.

Harvest. We ring on three. The tenor's home
in its frame, mouth down, rope wound round
its hook. Three bells sound thin. Still
a band, just about, and short of a hand.

## October

Clocks go back. The lost hour comes home.
She takes his rope down. Hold still,
I say. The bell does it. Let it come round
to you. Not the arm. Not even the hand.

She pulls. The bell goes up and holds still
on its stay. She lets it off. It swings home
and over. The sally comes to her hand.
She takes it, and brings the tenor round.

## November

Eleven. Two minutes. The village still.
Muffles on. Her job now. Her hand
on his rope. The names that never came home
are the names on the peal boards all round

the chamber walls. Open, hush. Her hand
won't fight it. Open, hush. Stand. The still
that follows is his, till the hill, round
the far side, gives it back. Then home.

## December

Christmas. Mince pies on the sill. Her hand
has a blister off the tenor. We ring round
for the ten o'clock. The lane is still
mostly in bed. Then everyone home.

New Year's Eve. She ties the muffles round
the clappers: his job, now hers. Her hand
is slow with cold. We wait below. At home
the village counts. The ropes hang still.

Look to. Treble's going. She's gone. Round
and over. The year goes out, hand over hand,
open, hush, open, hush. A minute still
in the dark. Then open. And four ways home.
