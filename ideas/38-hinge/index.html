Hinge

You are deciding something, and you believe a dozen things with varying confidence. Write them down as ranges and chances. Hinge works out which belief the decision actually turns on, what it would be worth to find out before choosing, and what would have to be true for you to be wrong.

      Writing formulas

      Use the assumption names. Arithmetic: + − * / ^ and parentheses. Numbers may carry k, M, B or %: 160k, 2.5M, 25%. Functions: if(test, a, b), min, max, abs, sqrt, ln, exp, round, floor, ceil, clamp(x, lo, hi). Tests: < > <= >= = !=, and, or, not.

      A chance is 1 when it happens and 0 when it does not, so if(exit, equity * exit_value, 0) reads the way you would say it. A formula row is a named intermediate; it can use any other row. Assumptions are drawn independently: if two of yours move together, derive one from the other with a formula row.

      How these numbers are computed

      Every range becomes a probability distribution: lognormal when it is positive (you are unsure to within a factor), normal otherwise, with your low and high as the 5th and 95th percentiles; uniform means hard limits. A chance is a coin with the weight you gave. Assumptions are independent. The engine draws — joint samples (Latin hypercube, seed —) and evaluates every option on each.

      Best is the option with the highest expected value (lowest, when smaller is better). Best in is the share of samples where that option comes out ahead. Value of perfect information is the mean of the per-sample best outcome minus the best mean outcome: what you would gain, on average, by choosing with full knowledge.

      For each uncertain assumption the engine then holds all the other samples fixed and re-evaluates the options with that one assumption pinned. The answer changes if… finds the values of the assumption at which the expected best option switches, by scanning the pinned expectation at 36 points (the medians of — equal-probability slices, plus the 0.1%, 5%, 95% and 99.9% points) and bisecting; where different options take over on different stretches, each is named. Chance is the probability your own distribution puts on those values. The scan can miss a switch region narrower than about one slice that falls between two scan points, and it does not look beyond the 0.1% and 99.9% points. Worth knowing is the value of learning which slice the assumption falls in (the outer slices cut finer, and any slice that holds a switch point cut there; its two states, for a chance): the average over slices of the best slice-mean, minus the best average. Because the best option does not change inside a slice, this is the exact partial value of information up to sampling noise, as long as the scan found the switch points; at 5,000 samples a mid-sized figure wobbles by about a sixth. Share of uncertainty is the variance of the slice means divided by the total variance of the best option's lead over the rest (or of the estimate), a first-order Sobol index; slicing keeps about 99% of it for a normal input or a lognormal one spanning a factor of ten. Swing is the pinned expectation at the 95th percentile minus the 5th.

      All of this is Monte Carlo, so the numbers wobble a little: re-roll to see how much. Values below half a percent of the total are shown as ≈0. Full definitions, limits and tests are in the README.
