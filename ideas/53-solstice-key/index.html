A continuous printed solid; the hour lines are a separate paper face. The golden line is an equinox shadow, drawn to the edge when its tip falls beyond the plate.

02 / MAKE IT LOCAL

STL units: millimetres. Paper: A4, actual size / 100%. No software is needed to use this workshop; a slicer is needed to print the STL.

03 / FROM FILE TO SHADOW

A little assembly.
A different kind of time.

  1. Print flat. Base on the bed, gnomon upwards. A 0.4 mm nozzle and 0.2 mm layers are starting assumptions. The solid shape needs no supports; choose infill in your slicer.
  2. Add the face. Print its SVG at 100%, measure the 100 mm calibration line, trim the square and cut the dashed slot. Slide it over the gnomon and attach it with a little glue.
  3. Find the pole. Level the base. The page’s top points to true north. The gnomon slopes toward the celestial pole: north in the northern hemisphere, south in the southern.
  4. Read the edge. In direct sunlight, read the polar sloping edge’s shadow against the hour lines. This reads local solar time, not your time-zone clock.

A dimensioned, geometrically checked prototype; not yet physically printed. Printer fit and outdoor performance remain untested. Keep the base level and do not use a magnetic compass reading without accounting for local declination.
