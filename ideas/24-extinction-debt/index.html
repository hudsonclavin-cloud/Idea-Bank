    The part that can be proven

    Every site is cleared, empty, or held by one individual. Species i sends propagules to every site at rate cipi; a propagule takes any site held by a worse competitor, dies on a better one; every individual dies at rate m. With pi the share of all land and D the share cleared:

    dpi/dt = cipi (1 − D − Σj≤i pj) − m pi − pi Σj<i cjpj

    The trade-off here is the geometric one: ci = m/(1−q)2i−1, which makes the undisturbed shares q, q(1−q), q(1−q)²… Rank k survives if and only if D < 1 − (1−q)2k−1, and nothing else about the clearing matters, not its shape, not where it is. The thresholds, with q = 0.2:

    Rank 1 is displaced by nobody, so its share obeys an exact logistic equation, dp/dt = p(r − c1p) with r = c1(1−D) − m = m(q−D)/(1−q). Cut just past the threshold and r is barely negative: the decline is glacial, and the species is doomed all the same.

    Each row's tick and the dashed forecast come from this equation set, integrated from the forest's state right now. "Gone by" is the year the forecast falls below one individual in the forest's 10,000 sites. In the stochastic forest the last tree usually dies earlier: with 40 % cleared, 20 runs lost it 805–1,785 years after the cut against a forecast of 1,324; with 30 % cleared, 1,217–1,970 years against 2,548, because a population lingering at a dozen trees for centuries rarely lasts. Rare species the model does not doom can also vanish by chance while the forest reshuffles. The tests in model.test.mjs check the mathematics; the README reports those runs.

    What you are looking at

    Ten thousand sites, ten species, one rule: a better competitor always wins a site, a worse competitor always spreads faster. That trade-off is what lets ten species share one patch of ground, and it is also the trap. The best competitor, #1, spreads only 1.25 times faster than it dies. To break even it needs four fifths of all land to be land it could ever occupy. Clear a fifth of the forest and that is gone.

    Nothing happens at once. The trees already standing are not told. Each dies in its own time, and each gap is a little more likely to be filled by something faster. The share of #1 slides toward zero along a curve whose time constant is 1/|r|: 800 years if you clear 25 %, 200 years if you clear 40 %, for trees that live 50 years. The species is not declining because it is rare. It is the commonest tree in the forest for most of that time.

    That is the debt. The land has already decided which species will go; the forest merely has not paid yet. Tilman, May, Lehman and Nowak gave it the name in 1994. The same mathematics says the payment can be refused: give the land back while the species still stands and its share climbs again. Give it back a year after the last tree and nothing comes back, because in this model there is no outside.

    Simplified on purpose: every site is identical, propagules reach every site equally (so a cleared strip and scattered clearings of the same area are the same), one individual per site, no seed bank, no immigration, competition strictly ranked. The README has the derivations, the sources and an honest list of what this does and doesn't capture.
